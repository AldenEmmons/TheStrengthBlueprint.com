# How We Work

> Alden's operational preferences, patterns, and what to avoid when building for The Strength Blueprint.

---

## Operating System: 3 Principles in Priority Order

1. **Load Management & Capacity Building** — The default lever. Intelligent load dosing over corrective drill prescription. When a client has pain, the first question is "what can they tolerate and how do we progress from there?" — not "what structural flaw do we correct?"
2. **Biopsychosocial Assessment** — Screen and reassess across all four domains at every touchpoint: mechanical/load capacity, sleep/stress/recovery, beliefs/fear-avoidance, training and life history. None of these can be skipped.
3. **Assessment as Data, Not Diagnosis** — PROs, capacity benchmarks, movement video, and observation are inputs, not diagnoses. Findings that don't connect to client symptoms or goals are not corrected.

When principles conflict, higher-priority wins.

---

## Delivery Model

- **Platform:** Trainerize for all programming delivery
- **Form review:** Async video — client submits, coach reviews and responds in-app
- **Communication:** In-app messaging for questions, symptom check-ins, autoregulation guidance
- **Structured touchpoints:** W0 (full onboarding), W4, W8, W12 (full reassessments + 30-min video), quarterly thereafter

---

## Remote Limitations — Acknowledge Them

**What we can't do:**
- Palpate or perform hands-on assessment
- Run special orthopedic tests or provocation testing
- Control video angle quality beyond standardized protocol guidance
- Fully eliminate self-report bias

**How we compensate:**
- Standardized video protocol: same angles, same distance, same movements every reassessment
- Capacity benchmarks weighted heavily — objective load tolerance is the most reliable remote signal we have
- Validated PROs over observation — NPRS, PSFS, and region-specific PROs carry more diagnostic weight than movement observation in the online context
- Low referral threshold — when clarity isn't achievable remotely, the client gets referred for in-person assessment

---

## Assessment Preference

In the online context: **PROs and capacity benchmarks outweigh movement observation.**

Static postural observation has poor predictive validity for pain and injury risk. We record it as a baseline reference, but it does not drive programming. What the client reports (NPRS, PSFS) and what they can objectively load and perform (capacity benchmarks) are the primary programming signals.

---

## Programming Preference

- **Load management over corrective drills.** When uncertain, reduce load and progress — don't add another corrective.
- **Correctives must earn progression.** Every corrective prescribed needs: a clear target capacity, a measurable progression, a graduation criterion, and a connection to client goals. No activation theater.
- **No 2×10 drills with no criteria.** If a corrective can't be progressed or graduated, it doesn't belong in the program.
- **Consider all 4 biopsychosocial domains** when designing programs. A high-stress, poor-sleep client needs different load choices than a well-recovered client — even with similar mechanical presentation.

### RIR Targets by Phase
| Phase | RIR Target | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Restore | 3–4 | Sub-threshold — client should feel very far from failure |
| Bridge | 2–3 | Moderate — loading builds capacity without excessive fatigue |
| Build | 1–2 | Progressive overload — approaching true training stimulus |

---

## Referral Culture

Low threshold. When in doubt, refer out.

**Referral triggers:**
- Any red flag symptom
- No measurable improvement after 4–6 weeks of appropriate programming
- Symptoms worsening despite 2+ weeks of reduced load
- New symptoms appearing during programming
- Presentation that cannot be safely assessed remotely

The cost of an unnecessary referral is a phone call. The cost of missing something we shouldn't manage is not recoverable.

---

## Claims Standard

- Never promise pain elimination
- Never promise specific timelines for symptom resolution
- All client-facing generated text (intros, closings, program notes) uses approved TSB language
- "Pain Is Not A Life Sentence" is a brand tagline — not a clinical outcome claim
- Approved: "build strength without breaking down," "train around pain not into it," "improve capacity," "resilience," "performance-ready," "assessment-driven," "criterion-progressed"
- Banned: "pain-free" as a guarantee, "fix," "cure," "heal," "treat," specific pain-resolution timelines
