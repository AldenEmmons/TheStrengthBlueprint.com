## The Three Phases (Always Use These Names)

- **Phase 1 — Restore**
- **Phase 2 — Bridge**
- **Phase 3 — Build**

Clients enter at the phase matching their clinical presentation. Progression is criterion-based, not time-based.

## Key Terms

### Clinical & Assessment

- **NPRS** — Numeric Pain Rating Scale. 0–10 validated measure for pain intensity. MCID (minimum clinically important difference) = 2 points. Used at rest and on provocative movements.
- **PSFS** — Patient-Specific Functional Scale. Client nominates 3 activities they find difficult; rates each 0–10. MCID = 2 points per item. Directly tracks what the client actually cares about.
- **PRO** — Patient-Reported Outcome. Any validated questionnaire measuring the client's own experience (pain, function, disability). Includes NPRS, PSFS, ODI, DASH, NDI, LEFS, KOOS.
- **PRO battery** — The set of PROs administered at onboarding and each reassessment. Same instruments, same protocol every time.
- **ODI** — Oswestry Disability Index. Region-specific PRO for low back complaints. Scored 0–100%; MCID ≈ 10%.
- **DASH** — Disabilities of the Arm, Shoulder and Hand. Upper extremity function PRO. Scored 0–100; MCID ≈ 10.
- **NDI** — Neck Disability Index. Cervical spine PRO.
- **LEFS** — Lower Extremity Functional Scale. Scored 0–80; MCID ≈ 9.
- **KOOS** — Knee Injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score. Knee-specific PRO.
- **PSS-4** — 4-item Perceived Stress Scale. Quick validated screen for stress load. Used in the sleep/stress domain at onboarding and reassessment.
- **Capacity benchmark** — A loaded, measurable test used to track objective progress. Examples: split squat reps at bodyweight, RDL load tolerance, push-up reps, overhead press hold. More reliable than movement observation in the online context.
- **Criterion-based progression** — Advancing phases by meeting specific outcome criteria (NPRS, PSFS, PRO thresholds), not by time elapsed. A client is ready for Phase 2 when the numbers say so — not when 4 weeks have passed.
- **Biopsychosocial model** — Framework recognizing pain is driven by mechanical, psychological, and social factors simultaneously. The four domains we assess: mechanical/load capacity, sleep/stress/recovery, beliefs/fear-avoidance, training and life history.

### Programming

- **RIR** — Reps in Reserve. How many reps the client could do before reaching failure. Used for autoregulation. Phase targets: Restore = 3–4 RIR | Bridge = 2–3 RIR | Build = 1–2 RIR.
- **RPE** — Rate of Perceived Exertion. Effort scale (typically 1–10). Related to RIR: RPE 8 ≈ 2 RIR. Used interchangeably with RIR for autoregulation.
- **MEV** — Minimum Effective Volume. The least training volume that drives adaptation. Anchor for Phase 2 (Bridge) volume.
- **MAV** — Maximum Adaptive Volume. Optimal training volume range — most adaptation per unit of fatigue. Target volume range in Phase 3 (Build).
- **MRV** — Maximum Recoverable Volume. The most volume a client can recover from. Exceeding MRV degrades performance and increases injury risk. Ceiling for Phase 3 programming.

### Pain Science & Behavior

- **Kinesiophobia** — Fear of movement. A key driver of pain chronicity. Addressed through graded exposure and Pain Neuroscience Education.
- **Fear-Avoidance** — The behavioral pattern where avoiding movement maintains or worsens pain over time. We address this directly rather than accommodating it.
- **PNE** — Pain Neuroscience Education. Education targeting pain beliefs, kinesiophobia, and catastrophizing. Reframes pain as a protective alarm rather than structural damage signal.
- **Graded Exposure** — Structured, progressive reintroduction to feared or avoided movements and loads. The primary behavioral intervention for kinesiophobia and fear-avoidance.
- **Cortical Inhibition** — Muscle inhibition driven by pain signals. Isometric loading is one tool used to restore neuromuscular output in the presence of pain.
- **Baseline** — The client's current symptom level. We track changes from baseline, not from zero.

## Approved Language

Use these in all client-facing copy, intro text, and closing notes:

- "Build strength without breaking down"
- "Train around pain, not into it"
- "Improve capacity"
- "Resilience"
- "Performance-ready"
- "Evidence-based"
- "Individualized"
- "Assessment-driven"
- "Criterion-progressed"
- "Build trust in movement"

**Note:** "Pain Is Not A Life Sentence" is acceptable as a brand tagline where context makes the aspirational framing clear. It is NOT a clinical outcome guarantee or a promise that the client's pain will resolve.

---

## Banned Language

Never use in marketing, client-facing copy, or generated program content:

- "Pain-free" as an outcome guarantee (e.g., "you'll be pain-free in 6 weeks")
- "Cure" — we don't cure conditions
- "Fix" — we don't fix structural problems (see also: "fix the root cause" — retired)
- "Heal" — implies a medical treatment claim
- "Treat" — outside CPT scope
- Specific timeframes for pain resolution ("pain gone in X weeks")
- Before/after pain claims without documentation

**Borderline:** "Pain-free" is OK as aspirational brand language when context makes goal-orientation clear — e.g., "building toward pain-free performance" in a brand context. Never as a promise or guarantee. When in doubt, use approved language instead.

---

## Tone Rules

- Direct and confident — we have a clinical framework and stand behind it
- Challenge what clients have been told, but don't dismiss their experience
- Specific over vague — cite criteria, measures, thresholds
- Never say "just rest it" or imply pain means stop
- Never promise outcomes we cannot guarantee
