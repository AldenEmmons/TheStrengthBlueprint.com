# The System

> The technical programming reference for TSB. All workout plans must follow this system.
> Assessment drives programming. Criteria drive progression. Evidence drives decisions.

---

## Assessment Protocol (6 Steps)

### Step 1 — Intake & History
Full subjective: training history, injury timeline, goals, biopsychosocial factors, red flag screen.

Collect: age, occupation, activity level, sitting hours, conditions, medications, surgeries, pain areas and detail (location, NPRS at rest and activity, duration, aggravating/relieving factors), training background, equipment access, goals and 90-day vision, barriers, preferred training time, sleep and stress baseline, final open-ended notes.

**Red flag screen:** Any positive finding (bowel/bladder changes, unexplained weight loss, night pain waking from sleep, saddle anesthesia, progressive neurological deficit) = immediate referral out. Do not proceed to programming.

### Step 2 — PRO Battery
Administer at onboarding and each reassessment. These travel digitally without loss of fidelity — they are the most reliable signal in the online delivery model.

- **NPRS** (Numeric Pain Rating Scale, 0–10): validated, minimal clinically important difference (MCID) = 2 points. Use for pain intensity at rest and on provocative movements.
- **PSFS** (Patient-Specific Functional Scale): client nominates 3 activities they find difficult; rates each 0–10 (0 = unable, 10 = no difficulty). MCID = 2 points per item. Directly tracks what the client actually cares about.
- **Region-specific PRO** (select based on primary complaint):
  - Low back: ODI (Oswestry Disability Index, 0–100%; MCID = 10%)
  - Shoulder/arm: DASH (Disabilities of the Arm, Shoulder and Hand, 0–100; MCID = 10)
  - Neck: NDI (Neck Disability Index)
  - Lower extremity (general): LEFS (Lower Extremity Functional Scale, 0–80; MCID = 9)
  - Knee: KOOS (Knee Injury and Osteoarthritis Outcome Score)

### Step 3 — Self-Recorded Movement Video Battery
Standard movements recorded by the client at home. Same movements, angles, and distance at every reassessment.

Standard battery: bodyweight squat, hip hinge (RDL pattern), single-leg balance, overhead reach, push-up. Add region-specific movements based on complaint (e.g., step-up for knee, wall slide for shoulder).

**Interpretation rule:** Movement video is data, not diagnosis. Static postural findings do not drive programming unless directly connected to client symptoms. Capacity benchmarks carry more weight than movement quality observation in the online context.

### Step 4 — Capacity Benchmarks
Region- and goal-specific loaded tests. These are the most objective remote signal available.

Examples: split squat reps at bodyweight (single-leg capacity), RDL load tolerance (hip hinge capacity), push-up reps (upper push capacity), overhead press hold (shoulder stability/capacity). Document weight, reps, or time. Retest at every reassessment with the same protocol.

### Step 5 — Sleep & Stress Quick Screen
- Single-item sleep quality: "Rate your sleep quality over the past week, 0–10 (0 = terrible, 10 = perfect)"
- PSS-4 (4-item Perceived Stress Scale): validated, fast, reliable for tracking stress load over time

Sleep and stress are non-negotiable biopsychosocial variables. A client with 4/10 sleep quality and high PSS-4 score is not ready for the same loading as a well-recovered client — even with identical mechanical presentation.

### Step 6 — Reassessment Cadence
Same protocol, every time. No improvised assessments.

- **W4, W8, W12:** Full PRO re-administration, re-recorded movement battery (same angles, distances), capacity benchmark retest, 30-minute video check-in, phase progression decision documented.
- **Quarterly thereafter:** Full PRO + capacity reassessment, goal review, program redesign if needed.

## Online Delivery Model

### Week 0 (Onboarding)
Intake form + full history + red flag screen | PRO battery (NPRS, PSFS, region-specific) | Self-recorded movement video battery | Capacity benchmarks | 60-minute video consult to set goals, explain the phase model, and establish expectations.

### Weekly
Trainerize program updates | Async video form review (client submits, coach reviews asynchronously) | In-app messaging: questions, symptom check-ins, autoregulation guidance.

### W4 / W8 / W12
Full reassessment per Step 1–5 above | 30-minute video check-in | Phase progression decision documented in writing.

### Quarterly
Full PRO + capacity reassessment | Goal review | Program redesign.

## Remote Limitations & Mitigations

| Limitation | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| No palpation or hands-on assessment | Capacity benchmarks weighted heavily — load tolerance is the most reliable remote signal |
| No special tests or provocation testing | PROs over observation — NPRS, PSFS, region-specific scores travel without fidelity loss |
| Movement quality limited to client-controlled video angles | Standardized video protocol — same angles, distance, attire, movements each reassessment |
| Reliance on client self-report (subject to bias) | Low referral threshold — when clarity isn't achievable remotely, client is referred for in-person assessment |

## Phase-by-Phase Programming

### PHASE 1 — RESTORE
**Goal:** Reduce pain, restore baseline capacity, build trust in movement.

**Dominant tools:**
- Graded exposure — start modified, progress progressively to full movement
- Isometric loading — for irritable areas, particularly useful in tendinopathy (strong evidence: Rio et al. 2015 showed isometrics reduced patellar tendon pain; replicated in Achilles and other tendinopathies). Isometrics allow loading without mechanical irritation at the point of failure.
- Mobility within capacity (not end-range stretching into pain)
- Pain Neuroscience Education — reframe pain as a protective alarm rather than structural damage; targets kinesiophobia and catastrophizing
- Sleep and stress hygiene coaching within CPT scope
- Loaded, progressed correctives (no activation theater — every corrective earns progression criteria)

**Programming approach:**
- Lower volume: 2–3 sets per exercise, 4–6 working exercises per session
- Sub-threshold loading: avoid reproducing pain above a manageable level; if pain spikes, back off load before backing off movement
- Trainerize program updated every 1–2 weeks based on client-reported tolerance
- No heavy compound bilateral loading until NPRS ≤ 3/10 for 14+ days without flare
- RIR target: 3–4 (very conservative)

**Phase graduation criteria (must meet ALL):**
- NPRS ≤ 3/10 on previously provocative movements
- Tolerates baseline programming for 14+ days without flare
- PSFS improved ≥ 2 points on key items
- Client demonstrates load-management understanding in own words

---

### PHASE 2 — BRIDGE
**Goal:** Re-introduce loaded compound movement at progressively higher intensity. Build movement-specific capacity.

**Dominant tools:**
- Tempo work (e.g., 3-1-2 eccentric-pause-concentric) — builds capacity and confidence at end-range
- Partial → full ROM progressions — systematic range-of-motion expansion as tolerance allows
- RIR/RPE autoregulation — clients self-regulate intensity using RIR targets; bridges the gap between over- and under-loading
- High-frequency, low-volume loading of previously irritable patterns
- Stretch-position loading bias — emerging evidence (Pedrosa et al. 2022, Kassiano et al. 2023) suggests that loading muscles in lengthened/end-range positions produces superior hypertrophy and may be safer for previously irritable structures than peak-contraction loading

**Programming approach:**
- MEV-anchored volume (Minimum Effective Volume — least volume that drives adaptation, per Israetel/Renaissance Periodization framework)
- Conservative intensity progression: 2–5% load increase per session when RIR target is comfortably exceeded
- Stretch-position bias where safe (e.g., incline dumbbell curl, deficit split squat, Romanian deadlift over straight-leg)
- RIR target: 2–3

**Phase graduation criteria (must meet ALL):**
- NPRS ≤ 2/10 across full training week
- Tolerates compound movement at 60–70% est. 1RM without 24-hour symptom return
- Region-specific PRO in functional range (e.g., ODI <20%, DASH <25, LEFS >65)
- Two consecutive consistent training weeks without regression

---

### PHASE 3 — BUILD
**Goal:** Drive client-specific outcomes — hypertrophy, strength, body composition, sport readiness.

**Dominant tools:**
- Periodized loading (linear, undulating, or block periodization per client profile)
- MEV → MAV → MRV progression across the program block:
  - MEV (Minimum Effective Volume): starting point — least volume that drives adaptation
  - MAV (Maximum Adaptive Volume): optimal volume range — most growth per unit of fatigue
  - MRV (Maximum Recoverable Volume): ceiling — most volume client can recover from; beyond this, performance degrades
- Stretch-mediated hypertrophy bias (emphasized more aggressively than in Bridge)
- Evidence-based intensity techniques (drop sets, mechanical drop sets, rest-pause) used conservatively

**Programming approach:**
- Standard hypertrophy/strength templates (Full Body, Upper/Lower, PPL) customized per client
- PROs continue at reassessments but become secondary to performance benchmark progression
- RIR target: 1–2
- Volume progresses week over week within a block; deload when MRV signs appear (performance regression, sleep disruption, motivation drop)

## Referral Triggers

Refer out immediately if:
- Any red flag symptom (bowel/bladder changes, unexplained weight loss, night pain, saddle anesthesia, progressive neurological deficit)
- No measurable improvement after 4–6 weeks of appropriate programming (PROs flat or worsening)
- Symptoms worsening despite 2+ consecutive weeks of reduced load
- New symptoms appearing during programming that weren't present at intake

When in doubt, refer. The cost of an unnecessary referral is a phone call. The cost of missing a red flag is not recoverable.
