## The 3-Principle Operating System

When principles conflict, the higher-priority one wins.

---

### PRINCIPLE 1 — Load Management & Capacity Building (HIGHEST PRIORITY)

The default lever is intelligent load dosing — not corrective drill prescription.

Most non-specific musculoskeletal pain responds to graded, progressive loading more reliably than to corrective exercises targeting structural findings. The evidence base here is strong: Lehman, Cook, Peter O'Sullivan, JP Caneiro, and Kieran O'Sullivan have each argued — from different clinical angles — that the loading approach matters less than the dose, tolerance, and progression. O'Sullivan's Cognitive Functional Therapy (CFT) framework emphasizes restoring meaningful, functional movement under load rather than chasing postural corrections. Caneiro's work on chronic low back pain consistently shows that graded exposure to loaded functional tasks outperforms avoidance and correction-first strategies.

In practice: when a client presents with pain, our first question is "what loads does this client tolerate, and how do we progressively build on those?" Not "what structural flaw do we correct?"

**Online amplifier:** We cannot palpate, perform special tests, or observe tissue response directly. This means we lean even harder on what the client tolerates and how capacity changes over time. Load tolerance is our most accessible and reliable signal.

---

### PRINCIPLE 2 — Biopsychosocial Assessment

Pain is rarely purely structural. It is never purely psychological. We assess and reassess across four domains because any one of them can drive or sustain symptoms:

1. **Mechanical / load capacity** — what loads provoke, what relieves, injury history, tissue tolerance
2. **Sleep, stress, recovery** — sleep quality (0–10), PSS-4 stress screen, recovery context
3. **Beliefs, fear-avoidance, catastrophizing** — kinesiophobia (Tampa Scale pattern), movement avoidance, what the client believes is "wrong" with them
4. **Training and life history** — training age, injury timeline, what has and hasn't worked

**Why this matters in online coaching:** When we can't assess tissue directly, we must assess the full system rigorously. A client reporting 6/10 back pain with poor sleep, high stress, and strong fear-avoidance beliefs needs a different program design than a client with the same pain report but good sleep, low stress, and no catastrophizing — even if the mechanical presentation looks identical.

Ignoring any one domain produces worse outcomes. Assessing all four gives us the map.

---

### PRINCIPLE 3 — Assessment as Data, Not Diagnosis

Movement videos, PROs, capacity benchmarks, and observation are inputs. They are not diagnoses.

Static postural observation has poor predictive validity for pain and injury risk. A client with significant forward head posture may be completely asymptomatic; a client with textbook alignment may be in constant pain. We use postural observation as a baseline reference, not a programming driver.

The corollary: **findings that don't connect to client symptoms or goals are not corrected for their own sake.** We do not prescribe scapular retraction exercises because a video shows "winging" if the client reports no shoulder symptoms and has no shoulder-related goal. Correction without clinical rationale is noise that wastes program capacity and erodes client trust.

## The Phase Model

Progression is criterion-based, not time-based. Clients can enter at Bridge or Build. Some loop back to Restore after a flare. Phase placement is reassessed at every structured touchpoint.

**RESTORE** — Reduce pain, restore baseline capacity, build trust in movement.  
The phase doing the most work. Graded exposure to previously avoided movements, isometric loading for irritable structures, education about pain science, sleep and stress hygiene, loaded and progressed correctives. Not activation theater.

**BRIDGE** — Re-introduce loaded compound movement at progressively higher intensity.  
Tempo work, partial to full ROM progressions, RIR-autoregulated loading (target 2–3 RIR), MEV-anchored volume, high-frequency low-volume loading of previously irritable patterns.

**BUILD** — Drive client-specific outcomes: hypertrophy, strength, body composition, performance.  
Periodized loading, MEV→MAV→MRV progression, stretch-mediated hypertrophy bias (emphasize end-range/lengthened positions), performance benchmarks as the primary signal.

## Corrective Exercise Philosophy

Correctives are loaded, dosed, and progressively overloaded like any other training stimulus. If it's worth doing, it earns progression.

**We do NOT prescribe:**
- Endless 2×10 activation drills with no progression criteria
- Findings-driven correctives that don't connect to client symptoms or goals
- Generic "fix your posture" interventions
- Drills based on theoretical structural concerns the client doesn't actually report

**Every corrective in a TSB program must have:**
1. A clear target capacity (what are we building?)
2. A measurable progression (how do we know it's working?)
3. A graduation criterion (when does the client exit this exercise?)
4. A connection to the client's actual goals

## Evidence Hierarchy

**Tier 1 — Drives decisions:** Systematic reviews, meta-analyses, high-quality RCTs, clinical practice guidelines.

**Tier 2 — Supports decisions:** Cohort studies, lower-quality RCTs, mechanistic studies with strong rationale (e.g., isometric loading for tendinopathy; stretch-mediated hypertrophy evidence from Pedrosa et al., Kassiano et al.).

**Tier 3 — Informs heuristics:** Expert opinion, framework-based reasoning (NASM CES, Anatomy Trains, DGS, etc.). Useful for filling gaps — not for driving clinical decisions.

**Tier 4 — Used cautiously:** Anecdote, single-case observation, social media content. Not a basis for programming.

We tell clients and ourselves what tier is driving each decision. Tier 3 reasoning dressed up as Tier 1 certainty is one of the most common failure modes in fitness and rehab.
